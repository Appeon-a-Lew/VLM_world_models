## TL;DR
21 VLMs watch 2-3 s fixed-camera videos and must output a metric size, velocity or acceleration for a target object, given one numeric physical prior as text; the best model (ChatGPT-5.1, 53.1 MRA) is below humans (55.6) and Qwen3-VL-2B scores 29.0. The diagnostic analyses show the supplied inputs are under-used: removing the video often barely changes the score, and scaling the given prior by α (which should scale the answer by α) drops MRA by ~70-80%, i.e. models anchor on typical real-world magnitudes.

## Setup / method
- **Input**: video (fixed camera, rigid translational motion) + text: one physical prior (object size for "Static" questions, or velocity/acceleration at a timestamp for "Dynamic" ones) + question naming the unit; models are told to "output only the numerical answer and unit".
- **Output**: one number (size in m, velocity m/s, acceleration m/s²) for a possibly different target object at a timestamp. Solving requires: measure in pixels → get scale from the prior → convert the target quantity.
- **Categories**: 2D vs 3D motion relative to the camera × Static vs Dynamic prior (2S, 2D, 3S, 3D).
- **Data**: 569 unique videos, 3,355 questions; sources: Blender simulation (micro to astronomical scales), lab multi-view stereo + depth capture (free fall, sliding, pendulum, bouncing), internet videos with a known-size reference, and SAM-segmented versions on plain backgrounds.
- **Metric**: Mean Relative Accuracy (MRA, from VSI-Bench): fraction of tolerance thresholds met by |ŷ−y|/|y|, averaged. (The paper lists the threshold set inconsistently between §4.1 and App. A.2.) Up to 5 queries until a parseable number.
- **No training**: evaluation-only benchmark; zero-shot prompting, plus a CoT variant.
- **Diagnostics (§5, on a 2D-only subset of 161 instances)**: (i) *prior-only*: "the video is removed but the prompt ... is kept unchanged"; (ii) *counterfactual prior*: "multiplying the original physical prior by a scalar factor" α ∈ {0.001, 0.01, 0.1, 0.2, 5, 50, 100, 200, 500, 700}, video and question unchanged, scored against y_cf = α·y (valid because the kinematic answer is linear in the scale prior); (iii) 4-step CoT (pixels → scale → target pixels → world).

## Key results
- **Table 1 (avg MRA %)**: ChatGPT-5.1 53.1, Gemini-2.5 Pro 49.6, Gemini-2.5 Flash 48.6, Qwen3-VL-32B 46.0, Grok 4.1 45.0, InternVL-3.5-30B 40.7, Qwen3-VL-8B 38.8, ..., **Qwen3-VL-2B 29.0** (2S 27.1, 2D 39.0, 3S 17.6, 3D 32.1), InternVL-3.5-2B 25.0, Fuyu-8B 12.5; human 55.6. Monotone scaling within Qwen3-VL (29.0 → 38.8 → 46.0).
- **Counterfactual prior**: "most models' MRA drops by 80%", "even the strongest model drops by 70%"; interpreted as anchoring to typical magnitudes rather than using the given number.
- **Prior-only**: for several models (named: ChatGPT-5.1, Gemini 2.5 Pro/Flash, Qwen3-VL-32B) MRA without video is "often close to" the video+prior MRA; the authors call VLMs "powerful guessers conditioned on textual hints".
- **Table 2 (analysis subset; video+prior / prior-only / counterfactual / CoT), rows mapped by Table 1 order (tentative)**: row 1 (ChatGPT-5.1) 56.1 / 39.0 / 15.4 / 27.7; row 7 (Qwen3-VL-32B) 50.1 / 37.2 / 34.0 / 23.1; row 9 (Qwen3-VL-8B) 40.5 / 24.9 / 12.0 / 21.0; row 13 (Qwen3-VL-2B) 34.9 / 28.2 / 25.2 / 25.6. Note that on these numbers the 2B's prior-only gap (−6.7) and counterfactual drop (−28%) are small mainly because its baseline is already low; small models are less image-dependent *and* less prior-dependent — closer to uninformed.
- **CoT** mostly *hurts* (stated: only a few of 21 models improve; error propagates across steps).
- **Scene context**: multi-object scenes score higher than single-object ones; background complexity has a mild effect.

## Relevance to us
- **IDEA-008 / STATE open question "does exp000 use the stated impulse on val_ood_forces?"**: QuantiPhy's counterfactual-prior test is exactly the design we lack. Our impulse enters *only* through text (the t=0 image is pre-push), so it is a textual physical prior in their sense. IDEA-008 as written is observational (regress predicted |Δ| on impulse across episodes), which is confounded by object mass/size, distance to the table edge, direction, and the weak/strong mode split. The within-episode intervention — same image, impulse ×α — removes all those confounds (IDEA-025).
- **Our advantage over QuantiPhy**: their y_cf = α·y works only because their quantity is linear in the prior. Our displacement is nonlinear in impulse (Coulomb sliding: stopping distance ∝ v², plus table-edge clipping), so "scale the GT by α" is wrong for us; we should either test *sensitivity* (predicted displacement should grow ≈ α² for on-table sliders) or re-simulate counterfactual GT (we own the simulator).
- **Image-dependence gap**: QuantiPhy's prior-only ≈ video+prior is the failure our supervisor fears; exp000 already shows the *opposite* for the image (C-001: no-image/blank/swap all worse than static). QuantiPhy suggests the remaining risk for us is on the *text* side: the model may read the scene but ignore the number. An "action-dependence gap" is the natural twin of our image-dependence test.
- **Task B, mass is implicit**: our prompt gives impulse (N·s), not speed; speed = impulse/mass with mass inferred from visible size (density [400, 800] kg/m³, `configs/sim_train.yaml:10`). That is a QuantiPhy-style chain (prior → visual scale → target quantity), and their CoT result warns that spelling the chain out may not help a 2B model.
- **Qwen3-VL-2B baseline**: 29.0 MRA overall, 3D-static 17.6 — weak metric 3D reading zero-shot, consistent with our exp001 expectation of poor Task A.

## Ideas for us
- **IDEA-025 Counterfactual impulse scaling (eval-only, exp000 adapter)**: keep the image and everything else fixed, multiply the impulse number in the action text by α ∈ {0.5, 2} (chosen so most scaled speeds stay inside train [0.3, 1.6] m/s; drop or flag episodes whose scaled speed leaves it). Read out the target object's predicted displacement d̂(α) = ‖pred_B − own pred_A‖ and report the median log-log slope log(d̂(2)/d̂(0.5)) / log 4, separately for on-table sliders (boxes/cylinders) and balls, on val_id and val_ood_forces. Expected: physics-consistent use ⇒ slope ≈ 2 for sliders that stay on the table (d ∝ v²); prior/anchoring ⇒ slope ≈ 0; partial use ⇒ 0 < slope < 2. Single change: the impulse number in the prompt. Cost: worktree prompt option + ~0.15 GPU h. Note: counterfactual predictions have no GT unless re-simulated; they must not be written to `pred/normal.jsonl`, and if written as derived pred files the pinned scores against original GT are meaningless — log the slope to job.log instead. Re-simulated counterfactual GT would turn this into a scored test but needs an `op_gen` change (human).
- **IDEA-026 Counterfactual direction flip (eval-only, exp000 adapter)**: same image and impulse, swap the direction phrase to its opposite (left↔right, away↔toward). Report the fraction of target objects whose predicted displacement projected on the *stated* camera-frame direction is positive, original vs flipped. Expected: if the model reads the action, the sign follows the flip in ≳90% of cases; if it infers direction from the scene layout (e.g. `aim_at_other_prob: 0.4` makes "toward another object" predictable from the image), the flip is partly ignored — a scene-prior shortcut. Cost: same job as IDEA-025, +0.05 GPU h.

## Caveats / disagreements
- Their prior is a *scale* given in text and the answer is linear in it; ours (impulse) maps nonlinearly to displacement and also requires mass inference, so their "80% drop" has no direct analogue — we need slope/sensitivity, not MRA vs α·y.
- Their α range (0.001-700) is wildly out of distribution; the drop partly measures refusal to output implausible magnitudes, not only prior anchoring. For us α should stay near the training range first, then go OOD (val_ood_forces speeds are 0.08-0.25 and 2.0-2.8 m/s, i.e. α ≈ 0.25 and ≈ 2 of typical).
- Zero-shot only; no fine-tuned models. Our exp000 is SFT'd on the action format, so text-prior behaviour may differ completely.
- Table 2 rows are unlabelled in the HTML; the per-model mapping above assumes Table 1 order. Prompt templates (App. F) not read.
- 161-instance subset, 2D-only, for the diagnostics; the authors call these analyses diagnostic.
