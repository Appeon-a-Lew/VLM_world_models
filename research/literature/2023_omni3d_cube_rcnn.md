## TL;DR
Omni3D merges six existing 3D datasets (KITTI, nuScenes, SUN RGB-D, ARKitScenes, Hypersim, Objectron) into one benchmark: 234,152 images and 98 categories, all in a shared camera frame (+x right, +y down, +z inward, metres). Cube R-CNN predicts each box as a projected 2D centre plus depth and back-projects with the known K. It handles mixed intrinsics with a *network-side* "virtual depth" target z_v = z·(f_v/f)·(H/H_v), with f_v = H_v = 512, which adds +6.0 AP3D and makes scale augmentation possible.

## Setup / method
- **Data unification (App. A1)**: the camera centre is the origin, "+x facing right, +y facing down, +z inward". Each label has a category, a 2D box, a 3D centroid "in camera-space meters", a 3×3 object-to-camera rotation, and w/h/l in metres. Calibration is known for every image. The dataset labels are **metric**. I found no statement that the annotations themselves are rescaled to virtual depth.
- **Sources (Table 6)**: KITTI (outdoor, 7,481 imgs), SUN RGB-D (indoor, 10,335), nuScenes (outdoor, 34,149), **Objectron ("general", 46,644 imgs, 9 categories)**, ARKitScenes (indoor, 60,924), Hypersim (indoor, 74,619). Omni3D_IN = 145,878 images and Omni3D_OUT = 41,630. "Objectron, with primarily close-up objects, is used only in the full Omni3D setting" (§3.1). The text I could read does not name Objectron's categories or give its depth or object-size statistics. App. A1 gives input resolutions of "370 to 1920" and focal lengths "from 518 to 1708 in pixels". These digits might be affected by the rendering artefact.
- **Cube R-CNN head (§4.1)**: per RoI it predicts [u, v], "the projected 3D center on the image plane relative to the 2D RoI", plus depth z. The 3D centre X is back-projected from [u, v] and z using the RoI box, the "known focal lengths" (fx, fy) and the principal point. It also predicts dimensions, rotation and an uncertainty.
- **Virtual depth (§4.2, App. A2.2)**: "If z is the true metric depth of an object from an image with height H and focal length f", its virtual depth is z_v = z·(f_v/f)·(H/H_v). f_v and H_v are hyperparameters (f_v = H_v = 512, §5). f and H are pixel quantities (the A2.2 proof works in pixels). Because f/H does not change when an image is resized, z_v depends only on the vertical FoV. The network regresses z_v, and z is recovered by inversion, z = z_v·(f/f_v)·(H_v/H). The paper says depth is "transformed from virtual depth" but does not write out the inverse; the formula here is my algebra.
- **Augmentation (§5)**: horizontal flips and random scales in [0.50, 1.25], described as "enabled by virtual depth".
- **Unknown-intrinsics fallback (App. A8, COCO demo)**: the authors assume f = 2·H, p = image centre, and predictions are then "real-world up to a scaling factor".

## Key results
- **Virtual-depth ablation (Table 1, Omni3D test, AP3D)**: full model 23.3; without virtual depth 17.3 (−6.0, "improves AP3D by +6%", §5.1); without scale augmentation 20.2; without scale aug and without virtual depth 19.8. The text also says scale augmentation *without* virtual depth is harmful ("−2.5%"). I could not reconcile that figure with the Table 1 rows I could read, so treat it as unverified.
- **Virtual camera added to baselines (Table 2, "+vc")**: +0.5 to +4.2 AP3D (PGD +4.2 on Omni3D).
- **Per-dataset (Tables 2, 5)**: Cube R-CNN Omni3D 23.3, Omni3D_OUT 31.9. When trained on Omni3D_IN: Hypersim 17.8, SUN RGB-D 35.4, ARKitScenes 41.2. Omni3D_OUT: KITTI 42.4, nuScenes 39.0. **No Objectron-specific AP3D is reported** in the text I read.
- **Limitation (§6)**: the method "is limited by the assumption of known camera intrinsics".

## Relevance to us
- **The camera frame matches ours exactly**: OpenCV, +x right, +y down, +z forward, metres. This confirms the frame row of `2025_qwen3_vl.md`. A sign or axis bug in exp001 cannot come from the Omni3D convention.
- **Does Omni3D contain tabletop data?** Partly. Objectron is 46,644 images of "primarily close-up objects", the only non-room-scale source. Even so, it is not our regime of 5–12 cm primitives on a plain table, and the Qwen3-VL report names neither Objectron nor any other training source. Qwen evaluates only on ARKitScenes / Hypersim / SUN RGB-D (the Omni3D_IN sources). That is a weak hint that the indoor part was used, and says nothing either way about Objectron. Zero-shot tabletop competence therefore cannot be assumed from Omni3D.
- **What "virtual camera" would mean for Qwen3-VL outputs (IDEA-024)**. In Omni3D, virtual depth is part of the *model's prediction target*. It is not part of the benchmark labels, which stay metric. Qwen's phrase "follow Omni3D ... to unify all data into a virtual camera coordinate system" can therefore mean either (a) metric labels in Omni3D's shared frame, or (b) Cube R-CNN-style virtual-depth labels. The paper cannot settle this. If (b), my derivation gives:
  - The virtual camera has focal f_v·H/H_v = H in original pixels. A point that projects to pixel (u, v) with true depth z gets z_v = z·H/f. Its lateral coordinates are X_v = (u−cx)·z_v/H = (u−cx)·z/f = X, and likewise Y_v = Y. **So only z is rescaled, and x, y stay metric.**
  - For our camera (448×448, fovy 50°): fy = 224/tan 25° ≈ 480.4 px, so f/H ≈ 1.072. Under (b), z_metric = 1.072·z_pred with x, y unchanged. At our object depths of roughly 0.9–1.3 m, that is a bias of about 0.06–0.09 m in z. Because K is constant across all episodes and splits, the bias is the same everywhere.
  - Projecting a (b)-style prediction with *our* K moves it radially outward by a factor of 1.072 about (cx, cy), i.e. ~11 px at 150 px from centre. Projecting with K_v (fx = fy = 448) puts it back on the correct pixel. This gives a test that does not need any depth GT fitting (added to IDEA-024).
- **Should a depth rescale be applied to Qwen3-VL outputs given our K? No, not by default.** (1) The evidence for (b) is only an inference from a citation. (2) Even if (b) holds, the correction is at most ~7% of z (≤ ~0.09 m), which cannot explain the ~0.7 m smoke-test error. (3) The exp001 metric was pre-registered on raw outputs. Recommendation: keep the raw outputs as the primary exp001 number, and report a z×1.072 (x, y unchanged) variant only as a labelled post-hoc diagnostic. Treat it as the convention only if IDEA-024 finds a fitted z-scale near 1.07, x/y scales near 1, and lower pixel error under K_v than under our K. For SFT models (exp000 and later) the question does not arise, because K is constant and the LoRA absorbs any constant scale.
- **val_ood_config**: the virtual-depth trick removes *intrinsic* variation only. Our shift is extrinsic (elevation 12–25° vs 30–60°) with fixed K, so virtual depth does nothing for it. Omni3D has no gravity- or elevation-aware component in the parts I read.
- **IDEA-014 / IDEA-019**: Cube R-CNN's centre parameterisation (projected 2D centre + depth, back-projected with known K) is IDEA-014's (u, v, z) output, at detector scale. Its ablations show that when intrinsics vary, the depth target has to be made K-consistent (virtual depth +6.0 AP3D) before scale augmentation helps. That bears directly on how IDEA-019 crops should be supervised.

## Ideas for us
No new IDEA number. Both points extend existing ideas:
- **Extend IDEA-024** with the virtual-camera prediction: z-only scale of f/H ≈ 1.072 with x, y unchanged, plus the projection test with K_v vs our K. Expected: this decides the convention question with no fitting freedom. Cost: unchanged (CPU, ~0.5 day).
- **Extend IDEA-019** with a control arm. If crops are added while the model may not read text K, also train a variant whose depth target is K-invariant virtual depth z·H/f′, converted back analytically with the crop's K. Omni3D reports that scale augmentation is harmful without a K-consistent depth target. Cost: the same as one extra IDEA-019 SFT run (~1.4 GPU h). Relevant only if IDEA-019 is planned.

## Caveats / disagreements
- The PDF could not be fetched (size limit), so the numbers come from the HTML render, which has digit-duplication artefacts. The focal-length range and the "−2.5%" scale-augmentation figure are the least certain.
- Omni3D is a detector benchmark scored by AP3D at IoU thresholds. It reports no metric centre error in metres.
- Omni3D/Cube R-CNN assume known K at train and test time. Qwen3-VL never receives K, which is a different regime: a VLM trained on virtual depth would have to infer the FoV from the image or assume it.
- Whether Qwen3-VL's 3D labels are metric or virtual is **not** established by this paper. Everything in "Relevance" about case (b) is conditional.
